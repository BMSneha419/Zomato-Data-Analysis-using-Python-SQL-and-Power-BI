# Zomato-Data-Analysis-using-Python-SQL-and-Power-BI

---

## 📌 Project Overview
This repository hosts an end-to-end data analytics workflow designed to evaluate operational performance, supply chain bottlenecks, and customer sentiment for food delivery operations across **98 Indian cities**. Processing a massive footprint of **44,891 unique restaurant listings**, this project moves away from isolated reporting layers and introduces a unified **three-tier data architecture**. 
The primary business objective is to uncover the hidden operational levers that drive up consumer sentiment, eliminate delivery latency, and optimize multi-city marketplace performance.

---

## 🛠️ Technology Stack
* **Data Engineering & ETL Processing:** Python 3.x | Pandas | NumPy
* **Data Warehousing & Analytics Engine:** SQL (PostgreSQL) | Advanced Window Functions & CTEs
* **Business Intelligence & Insights:** Power BI Desktop | Advanced DAX Modeling

---

## ⚙️ Phase-Wise Implementation Breakdown

### 🔹 Phase 1: Data Engineering & Preprocessing (Python)
The foundation of the pipeline focuses on programmatic data ingestion, cleaning, and structured feature engineering using **Python (Pandas)**.
* **Schema Enforcement & Exception Handling:** Standardized incoming data types, resolved structural text anomalies, and managed casting errors—such as handling dirty string flags when converting non-numeric text to numeric float variables.
* **Feature Engineering:** Built business-specific categorical definitions to prepare the data for advanced downstream slicing. This involved mapping out `Price_Category` tiers (Low, Medium, High), establishing `Delivery_Speed_Category` limits (Fast, Moderate, Slow), and implementing tracking fields like `Is_Multi_Cuisine` and `Safety_Flag`.

### 🔹 Phase 2: Relational Database Modeling (SQL)
With clean data generated by Python, the datasets were migrated into an SQL relational database to run performance-optimized analytical transformations via structured scripts.
* **Modular Logic via CTEs:** Avoided highly nested subqueries by wrapping complex multi-table joins and aggregation paths inside clean, reusable Common Table Expressions.
* **High-Resolution Window Functions:** Deployed advanced analytical operations like `DENSE_RANK()`, `AVG() OVER()`, and partition matrices to segment restaurant performance, isolate regional delivery speeds, and score city-level delivery bottlenecks without incurring steep front-end processing overhead.

### 🔹 Phase 3: Business Intelligence & Interactive Dashboarding (Power BI)
The optimized relational views were imported directly into a specialized, **4-page interactive reporting suite** within Power BI (`Zomato data analysis.pbix`).
* Tracked high-level marketplace status: `44,891` active vendors maintaining a `3.92 / 5.0` star baseline, a `33-minute` average delivery cycle, and an average ticket value of `₹173` for two.
* Maped major volume clusters showing high restaurant density led by **Kolkata** (`1,296`), **Kanpur** (`1,185`), and **NCR** (`1,128`).
* Isolated customer review segments, highlighting `23,502` moderately rated and `10,327` highly rated operators.
* Evaluated delivery operational speeds: `32,305` Moderate speed providers, `8,978` Slow logistics profiles, and `3,608` elite Fast fulfillment nodes.
* Isolated the specific elements that drive positive customer reviews: Standard units combining `Fast Speed + Premium Pricing + Safety Compliance` yield a peak platform rating of **`4.08`**, while slow fulfillment drags average customer satisfaction down to **`3.88`**.
* Evaluated localized delivery dynamics across geographic boundaries: Highlights high-efficiency markets like **Kolkata** (scaling `1,296` storefronts at a fast **`44.75-minute`** delivery average) against infrastructure-restricted zones like **Motihari** (averaging a slower **`52.36-minute`** cycle time and a lower **`3.81`** rating).
* Exposed back-end kitchen operational stress: Proves that extreme menu diversification slows down logistics riders, where complex multi-cuisine mixes (e.g., *Chinese, Maharashtrian, Malwani, Seafood, Biryani*) peak at a long **`93.0-minute`** average delivery cycle.

## 🎯 Conclusion & Strategic Recommendations

The data processed across this modern data stack workflow delivers several clear, actionable takeaways for operational optimization:

1. **Fulfillment is the Primary Engine of Customer Satisfaction:** Customer ratings are heavily dependent on delivery speed and safety compliance. By keeping safety protocols active and shifting lagging delivery partners into faster speed brackets, the platform can expect an approximate **4% to 5% uplift** in overall customer satisfaction.
2. **Standardize Complex Menu Handshakes:** Complex multi-cuisine combinations create severe bottlenecks in kitchen preparation time, pushing delivery times past **90 minutes**. The platform can address this by implementing standardized kitchen-prep limits or introducing staggered order acceptance windows for complex menu profiles to protect the logistics pipeline.
3. **Deploy Targeted Regional Playbooks:** Instead of applying identical operational rules across all areas, the platform should use localized strategies. This means rolling out **Expansion Playbooks** to replicate high-speed, high-performing hubs (such as Agra's elite fast tier), while utilizing **Operational Correction Playbooks** in volume-heavy but slow-moving delivery hubs to eliminate latency and hit the corporate target rating baseline of **`4.00`**.
